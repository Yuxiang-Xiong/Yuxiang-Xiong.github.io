Political economy and trade

Do greener parliaments change trade?

Close elections offer a way to study political representation and its possible connections to market access.

Working paper · Research as of October 2026

The question

Does greater Green representation in European parliaments change the conditions faced by Chinese exporters? Parliamentary representation provides a specific political link between elections and trade.

What this study does

The project studies 63 national elections in 13 countries, comparing elections close to reconstructed, approximate Green seat thresholds. It examines representation and exports separately, alongside tariff and product-level non-tariff-measure checks.

What it finds

Pooled local estimates show a positive discontinuity in observed Green seat share. The separately estimated export discontinuity is negative but imprecise, and fixed-window comparisons yield different patterns.

A pooled tariff estimate is negative, while separate product-level checks provide little support for rising non-tariff barriers. These results do not support a simple story in which greater Green representation consistently raises barriers and reduces exports.

Why it matters

Environmental politics can affect market access through several channels. Distinguishing seats, policies, and trade outcomes helps make the proposed connection testable rather than assuming that they move together.

Interpreting the evidence

The thresholds are reconstructed approximations and the design remains exploratory. Results depend on the comparison window and specification. An observed change in parliamentary representation does not by itself establish a precise causal effect on exports.

The paper

Green Parliamentary Representation and Chinese Exports: A Seat-Threshold Study

Abstract

This paper studies Green parliamentary representation and Chinese exports to Europe through an exploratory seat-threshold design covering 63 national elections in 13 countries. Comparisons close to reconstructed, approximate Green seat thresholds yield a positive pooled local discontinuity in observed Green seat share. The separately estimated export discontinuity is negative and imprecise. Fixed-window estimates with country and election-year effects yield different patterns. A pooled tariff estimate is negative, while separate product-level non-tariff-measure checks provide little support for rising barriers. The study examines parliamentary representation as a political link between elections and trade, retaining uncertainty about export and policy effects.

Methods and data

  • European elections and parliamentary representation; Chinese exports by destination and six-digit Harmonized System product.
  • Reconstructed seat thresholds; local and fixed-window comparisons; tariffs and non-tariff measures from the United Nations Conference on Trade and Development Trade Analysis Information System.
  • Green parties
  • Political representation
  • International trade
