Agriculture and biodiversity

Can pesticide rules help farmland birds?

A national restriction may matter differently across crops, places, and bird communities.

Working paper · Research as of October 2026

The question

China restricted the use of chlorpyrifos and triazophos on vegetables. Do places growing more vegetables show different changes in bird records, and are the changes strongest among birds linked to farmland?

What this study does

The study combines bird observations from 2013 to 2019 with county crop exposure. A triple-difference design compares sensitive bird groups with other groups, before and after enforcement, across counties with different vegetable shares.

The main panel covers 488 counties. Farmland and open-country birds are linked to agricultural habitats; insect-eating birds may also be exposed through their food supply.

What it finds

Counties with larger vegetable shares show greater relative post-enforcement increases in sensitive-group observed richness, recording frequency, and reported bird counts. The clearest estimates concern farmland and open-country birds. Estimates for insect-eating birds alone are positive but imprecise.

Why it matters

The ecological relevance of a pesticide rule depends on where regulated crops are grown and which birds interact with those landscapes. The study connects these dimensions instead of treating all birds and farmland alike.

Interpreting the evidence

These are observed bird records, which also depend on recording activity. The comparisons do not establish nationwide bird-population recovery. Policy timing, crop-exposure measurement, and the observation process remain important to interpretation.

The paper

Pesticide Restrictions, Vegetable Cultivation, and Bird Diversity: Evidence across Functional Groups in China

Abstract

This paper studies China's restrictions on chlorpyrifos and triazophos use on vegetables using bird observations from 2013 to 2019. A triple-difference design compares changes in sensitive bird groups relative to other groups before and after enforcement across counties with different vegetable shares. In a panel of 488 counties, higher vegetable exposure is associated with larger post-enforcement relative increases in observed richness, recording frequency, and reported bird counts. The clearest estimates concern farmland and open-country birds; insectivore-only estimates are positive but imprecise. The findings connect crop structure and birds' ecological links to the regulated use, while retaining the distinction between observed diversity and population recovery.

Methods and data

  • Chinese bird observations; county crop exposure from the Spatial Production Allocation Model.
  • County-year-functional-group panel; triple-difference comparisons and event windows.
  • Pesticide regulation
  • Farmland birds
  • Agriculture
